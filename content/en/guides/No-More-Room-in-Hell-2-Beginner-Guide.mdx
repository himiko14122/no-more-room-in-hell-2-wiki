## Your First Deployment: What to Do First as a Responder

Every match begins with a **CRC** briefing that assigns your squad a primary objective. As a new **Responder**, your first task is never to charge the nearest building. Open your map, mark the objective waypoint, and check your squad's loadouts. The official [NMRIH2 Beginner Survival Guide](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/292000) emphasizes four core loops: listen for zombie audio cues, manage inventory weight, learn early extraction routes, and conserve ammunition. If you are searching for no more room in hell 2 what to do first guidance, the answer is always the same: secure your loadout, listen for audio cues, and move as a squad. This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide follows that same philosophy.

### The CRC Briefing and Objective Flow

Your **CRC** handler issues objectives like restoring power, clearing a safe room, or locating supply caches. Each completed objective grants **Credits** and **Merits**, which feed into account-wide progression. For a full breakdown of mission types, check our [objectives explained](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-objectives-explained/) article. The key takeaway: objectives are sequential, and skipping one usually locks the extraction point. Stay with the squad and complete objectives in order rather than wandering off to loot. A lone **Responder** who triggers a horde event early can doom the entire run.

### Audio Cues: The Most Important Survival Tool

Zombies telegraph their presence through distinct audio. **Walker**s produce a low shuffle, **Runner**s have heavy panting, and **Shambler**s let out a piercing scream before charging. If you hear any of these sounds, stop moving and identify the direction. The [Tutorial Gym walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5XVhjuDPb5I) demonstrates how audio awareness alone prevents most ambushes. This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide recommends playing with headphones at all times, because visual cues alone are unreliable in dark areas like **Flooded**. The moment you hear a **Shambler** scream, your priority shifts from looting to positioning.

## Core Movement and Stamina Mechanics

Movement is the difference between life and death in No More Room in Hell 2. The stamina bar governs sprinting, melee swings, and even jumping. Overencumbering your inventory directly reduces stamina regeneration, turning you into a walking target. Learning how to play no more room in hell 2 effectively starts with mastering this movement system, and this no more room in hell 2 beginner guide covers it in depth because most early deaths come from poor positioning, not poor aim.

### Inventory Weight and Encumbrance

Every item you carry has a weight value. A full combat load with a primary firearm, sidearm, melee weapon, throwables, and medical supplies can push you past the encumbrance threshold. When you exceed 75% of your max carry weight, your sprint drains stamina twice as fast and your melee swing speed drops noticeably. Drop duplicate items or stash them in supply crates. The table below shows the exact penalties you will face at each carry threshold.

| Carry Load | Sprint Duration | Melee Swing Speed | Stamina Regen |
|------------|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Under 50% | Normal | Normal | Normal |
| 50-75% | 80% duration | 90% speed | 75% rate |
| Over 75% | 50% duration | 70% speed | 50% rate |

### Sprint, Crouch, and Vault Timing

Sprinting is loud. Zombies within 15 meters will aggro on your footsteps, so only sprint when you have a clear path. Crouching reduces your noise radius to roughly 3 meters and lets you slip past **Walker** groups. Vaulting over obstacles costs stamina but breaks line of sight with **Runner**s. Practice these three movement states in the Tutorial Gym before your first real deployment. A **Responder** who masters crouch-walking through **Flooded** can complete objectives without firing a single shot.

## Best Beginner Loadouts and Weapons

Your starting loadout defines your early survivability. The official [NMRIH2 website](https://www.nmrih2.com) recommends first-time responders run a sidearm, a reliable melee, and a single throwable. This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide expands on that recommendation with three tested loadouts that balance damage, weight, and utility. Each loadout below was tested against **Walker** hordes and **Runner** ambushes on **Raven Rock**.

### Sidearms: M1911, M9A3, and Gruber 922

The **M1911** deals 45 damage per shot and drops a **Walker** with two chest hits. The **M9A3** holds 17 rounds and is more forgiving for players with shaky aim. The **Gruber 922** is a compact pistol that fits into small inventory slots but struggles against armored **Shambler**s. For a deeper dive into weapon stats, read our [firearm guide](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-firearm-guide/). Your sidearm is your emergency tool, not your primary damage source.

### Melee Weapons: Crowbar, Fire Axe, and Sledgehammer

Melee is your primary tool in the early game. The **Crowbar** has a fast swing and can break barricades. The **Fire Axe** deals heavy damage with a longer reach. The **Sledgehammer** is the slowest option but one-shots a **Walker** on a headshot and stuns **Shambler**s. Never carry two melee weapons; the weight penalty is not worth the flexibility. A single **Sledgehammer** in the hands of a skilled **Responder** can hold a doorway indefinitely.

### Throwables and Medical Gear

A single **Molotov** clears a corridor of **Walker**s, while a **Satchel Charge** is ideal for objective rooms. **Phalanx pills** restore a small amount of health instantly but have a cooldown. Carry one medical item and one throwable in your early runs. The loadout table below summarizes the three recommended starting combinations.

## Enemy Types: Walker, Runner, and Shambler

Understanding your enemies is central to any no more room in hell 2 beginner guide worth reading. Each zombie type demands a different response, and treating every encounter the same way will get you killed. The Armageddon 1.0 update rebalanced enemy health pools, so memorize the current numbers before you deploy.

| Enemy | Health | Speed | Threat Level | Counter |
|-------|--------|-------|--------------|---------|
| Walker | 100 | Slow | Low | Melee headshot |
| Runner | 80 | Fast | High | Kite and sidearm |
| Shambler | 250 | Medium | Critical | Shotgun or Sledgehammer |

### Fighting Walkers in Groups

**Walker**s are slow but dangerous in numbers. A single **Walker** takes one headshot from the **M7A1** carbine or two body shots from the **M1911**. When facing a group of three or more, backpedal and use your melee weapon's horizontal swing arc. Never let a **Walker** grab you from behind; the animation locks you in place for two seconds, which is enough time for the horde to surround you. Use doorways and narrow corridors to force **Walker**s into single-file lines.

### Kiting Runners Instead of Trading Hits

**Runner**s sprint at you and lunge. Trading melee hits with a **Runner** is a losing proposition because their lunge has priority over your swing. Instead, kite them around obstacles, wait for their lunge to miss, then strike. The **Fierra DB12** shotgun is the best anti-**Runner** tool because its spread guarantees a hit even at point-blank range. On **Nightmare** difficulty, **Runner** spawns double, so mastering this kite-and-strike rhythm is mandatory.

## Progression: Credits, Merits, Gene Therapy, and Phalanx Pills

Progression in NMRIH2 is account-wide, meaning your unlocks persist across matches. This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide explains how to spend your currency efficiently so you do not waste rare resources on the wrong unlocks. Every extraction, even a failed one, contributes small amounts of **Credits** to your account.

### Spending Credits on Unlocks

**Credits** are earned by completing objectives and extracting successfully. They unlock new weapons, attachments, and cosmetic items in the armory. Prioritize unlocking the **M7A1** carbine and the **Fierra DB12** shotgun first; these two weapons cover most combat scenarios from mid-range suppression to close-quarters defense. The progression table below shows every currency and its best use.

| Currency | Earned From | Best Use |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| Credits | Objectives, extraction | Weapon unlocks |
| Merits | Challenge completion | Cosmetic unlocks |
| Gene Therapy | Rare drops, events | High-level XP boost |

### Gene Therapy and Responder Death

When your **Responder** dies, you lose that character permanently. **Gene Therapy** is a consumable that grants a new **Responder** bonus experience points, accelerating early progression. Wasting **Gene Therapy** on a low-level **Responder** is one of the most common beginner mistakes. Save it for a **Responder** that has already survived several extractions. The XP boost scales with your current level, so using it early yields minimal returns.

### Phalanx Pills and Medical Supplies

**Phalanx pills** are the only instant health item in the game. They restore 15 health over five seconds and can be used while moving. Stockpile them for emergency situations, but remember they do not cure **NPV infection**. Only a teammate with a revive syringe or a medical station can halt infection progression. An infected **Responder** who hides their status will eventually turn and attack the squad mid-objective.

Each map in NMRIH2 has unique hazards that change how you approach objectives. **Raven Rock** is a rural town with wide sightlines; use rooftops to spot **Walker** groups before they spot you. **Flooded** is a dark, waterlogged map where audio cues are muffled; rely on your minimap and stay close to your squad. **Nightmare** is a high-difficulty map with increased **Runner** spawns and limited ammunition. On **Nightmare**, the **Sledgehammer** becomes your best friend because ammunition is too scarce for sustained gunfights. ## Common Beginner Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Every new player makes the same errors. Based on official NMRIH2 community feedback, here are the top five no more room in hell 2 tips and tricks for avoiding them.

1. **Wasting Gene Therapy on low-level Responders** - Save it for high-level characters.
2. **Ignoring zombie audio cues** - Wear headphones and stop moving when you hear a **Shambler** scream.
3. **Overencumbering inventory** - Drop duplicate items and stash loot in supply crates.
4. **Fighting Runners instead of kiting** - Use obstacles and wait for their lunge.
5. **Breaking barricades unnecessarily** - Barricades keep zombies out; only break them when an objective requires it.

This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide cannot stress enough that survival is about resource management, not kill count. A **Responder** who extracts with zero kills but full health is more valuable than one who dies with 50 kills. The same logic applies to **NPV infection** management: an infected **Responder** is a liability to the entire squad, so communicate your status immediately and prioritize medical stations over loot rooms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is No More Room in Hell 2 hard for beginners?**

Yes, the game has a steep learning curve, but the Armageddon 1.0 update added the Tutorial Gym to teach core mechanics like movement, melee timing, and reloading. This no more room in hell 2 beginner guide recommends completing the tutorial at least twice before your first real deployment to build muscle memory.

**What is the best weapon for a new player?**

The **M9A3** sidearm paired with a **Crowbar** is the most forgiving loadout. The **M9A3** holds 17 rounds and reloads quickly, while the **Crowbar** has a fast swing speed for crowd control. Upgrade to the **M7A1** carbine once you earn enough **Credits** from successful extractions.

**Does Gene Therapy carry over after death?**

**Gene Therapy** is consumed the moment you use it and applies bonus XP to your new **Responder**. It does not persist after death, so only use it on a **Responder** you plan to play long-term. Wasting it on a fresh character is the fastest way to slow your progression.
