## Start in the Tutorial Gym Before Anything Else

The Armageddon 1.0 update added the **Tutorial Gym**, a dedicated training ground that every new Responder should complete before touching a live objective. It walks you through **WASD movement**, sprint stamina, melee swing timing, aim-down-sights (ADS), reloads, and equipment usage. Skipping it is the most common reason new players die in the first two minutes of a match. The gym takes less than fifteen minutes and gives you a safe environment to internalize the **nmrih2 controls guide** fundamentals without zombie pressure. For a step-by-step breakdown of every station, see the [Tutorial Gym walkthrough](/en/guides/nmrih2-tutorial/).

### Movement, Sprint, and Stamina Discipline

Your sprint meter drains fast and regenerates slowly. In the gym, practice short bursts between cover instead of holding shift across open ground. Every melee swing also costs stamina, so a panic attack on a **Walker** can leave you winded and unable to sprint from a **Runner**. The **nmrih2 early game guide** rule here is simple: never let your stamina drop below one full bar in contested areas. When you hear the low-stamina gasp, your Responder is already vulnerable, so break line of sight and recover behind cover before engaging anything else.

### Melee Swing Timing and ADS Fundamentals

Melee weapons have distinct wind-up and recovery frames. The **Sledgehammer** is slow but one-shots Walkers on a headshot, while the **Crowbar** swings faster but requires two hits. In the gym, practice stepping forward into your swing so the active frame connects at maximum reach. For firearms, ADS tightens spread significantly, but hip-fire is viable inside arm's reach. The **M7A1** carbine rewards controlled three-round bursts, while the **Fierra DB12** shotgun punishes missed pellets. Run the gym until both feel automatic; that muscle memory carries into every map and every difficulty.

## nmrih2 Survival Basics: Audio Cues and Awareness

The single most important skill in No More Room in Hell 2 is listening. Zombies broadcast their position long before they enter your line of sight, and the **nmrih2 survival basics** all revolve around acting on those cues. A **Walker** shuffles with dragging footsteps, a **Runner** pants audibly between sprint bursts, and a **Shambler** screams before its charge. If you hear any of these sounds, stop, identify the direction, and check your flanks before proceeding. The official [NMRIH2 Beginner Survival Guide](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/292000) on Steam lists this as the first core loop, and it is easy to see why: audio cues save more responders than any weapon.

### Audio Cue Cheat Sheet

| Zombie Type | Audio Cue | Threat Level | Recommended Response |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Walker | Slow shuffle, occasional groan | Low | Melee headshot or ignore |
| Runner | Loud panting, rapid footsteps | High | Kite into a chokepoint, then finish |
| Shambler | High-pitched scream before charge | Critical | Break line of sight, use throwable |

The **CRC** (Civilian Response Corps) field manual stresses that audio cues are more reliable than visual detection in dark interiors. The **Flooded** map in particular has heavy rain that masks footsteps, so you must rely on the distinct zombie vocalizations. This is a core part of any **nmrih2 gameplay guide** because it applies to every difficulty, from Normal to **Nightmare**.

### Inventory Weight and Stamina Penalties

Every item you carry adds weight, and crossing certain thresholds reduces your sprint speed and stamina regeneration. The **nmrih2 early game guide** recommendation is to keep your total weight under 70% of capacity. A **Sledgehammer** plus a primary firearm plus a sidearm plus **Phalanx pills** plus ammunition can easily push you into the penalty zone. Drop duplicate melee weapons, consolidate ammo types, and remember that **Phalanx pills** restore health but take up precious inventory slots. You should carry one stack of pills and one **Revive Syringe** at most in the early game.

## Best Starting Loadout for New Responders

Your first runs should follow a strict loadout formula: one reliable sidearm, one melee weapon, and one piece of throwable equipment. The official **nmrih2 early game guide** loadout recommendation is the **M1911** or **M9A3** sidearm, a **Crowbar** or **Fire Axe** melee, and a **Satchel Charge** or **Molotov** throwable. This combination covers close-range defense, silent takedowns, and emergency crowd control without overencumbering you. For deeper weapon math, check the [firearm guide](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-firearm-guide/).
