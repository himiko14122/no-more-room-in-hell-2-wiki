## What Is the nmrih2 Tutorial Gym?

The Armageddon 1.0 update introduced the Tutorial Gym, a purpose-built training ground that replaces the old text-based onboarding. This nmrih2 tutorial area is accessible directly from the main menu and walks new responders through every core mechanic in a safe, zombie-free environment. The gym is divided into stations, each testing a specific skill: movement, melee combat, firearms, equipment handling, and audio identification. Completing the full circuit takes roughly 15 minutes and rewards you with starting **Credits** to purchase your first loadout. The gym is designed to be repeatable, so you can return anytime to practice a specific mechanic before heading into a live deployment.

### Navigating the Tutorial Gym Stations

Each station in the Tutorial Gym focuses on a single skill, and the order matters. The movement station teaches WASD controls, sprint stamina management, and crouch-walking through tight spaces. The melee station trains swing timing with the **Crowbar** and **Fire Axe**, emphasizing the headshot multiplier that makes melee viable against **Walkers**. The firearms station introduces the **M7A1** carbine and **Gruber 922** pistol, covering aim-down-sights (ADS), recoil control, and reload canceling. The equipment station covers throwables like the **Satchel Charge** and **Molotov**, plus the revive syringe. Finally, the audio station plays zombie sound cues and asks you to identify which enemy type is approaching. The official [Tutorial Gym walkthrough video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5XVhjuDPb5I) shows the full circuit if you prefer watching before doing.

### From Tutorial to First Deployment

Once you finish the gym, you're ready for your first deployment. The game recommends starting on **Raven Rock**, the most forgiving map, before attempting **Flooded** or **Nightmare**. Your first deployment should focus on objectives, not kills. This nmrih2 tutorial philosophy — prioritize objectives over combat — is the single biggest difference between survivors and corpses. New responders who chase every gunshot they hear usually end up surrounded. Stick to the objective marker, move as a unit, and conserve ammunition. Every objective you complete awards **Credits**, which directly translate into better gear for future runs. Don't be afraid to extract early if your squad is low on health or supplies; a successful extraction beats a heroic death every time.

## Core Mechanics Every Responder Must Master

Every **Responder** in No More Room in Hell 2 relies on a shared set of mechanics that the nmrih2 tutorial teaches. Mastering these fundamentals will carry you through every difficulty level, from standard deployments to the brutal **Nightmare** modifier. The four pillars are audio awareness, weight management, stamina discipline, and barricade integrity. Ignore any of these, and the zombie horde will punish you quickly. These mechanics aren't optional extras — they're the difference between a clean extraction and a respawn screen.

### Audio Cues and Zombie Awareness

Zombies in NMRIH2 are loud, and that's by design. **Walkers** shuffle with a distinctive foot-drag that sounds like sandpaper on concrete. **Runners** pant heavily as they close distance, giving you roughly two seconds to react. **Shamblers** emit a guttural scream before charging, which serves as both a warning and a threat display. The nmrih2 tutorial gym includes an audio identification station where you can practice recognizing these cues in isolation. Sound is your primary early-warning system, especially in dark corridors where visibility drops to near zero. If you hear a Runner but can't see it, assume it's already flanking you. Headphones aren't optional — they're the most important piece of equipment in your kit. The [Steam beginner survival guide](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/292000) lists audio cue recognition as the first core loop every new player must master.

### Inventory Weight and Stamina Management

Every item you carry adds weight, and every pound reduces your stamina regeneration. The **CRC** (Combat Readiness Check) system tracks your encumbrance level in real time. Staying under 50% weight keeps your sprint efficient and your dodge responsive. Overencumbered responders tire quickly, and a tired Responder is a dead Responder. The nmrih2 tutorial teaches you to drop non-essential items before engaging. That spare **Molotov** might feel useful, but if it pushes you over the weight threshold, it's a liability. Learn to read your **CRC** meter and adjust your inventory before every fight. Ammo management ties directly into this: carry enough for one engagement, then resupply at supply crates instead of hoarding boxes in your pack.

## Best Loadouts for New Responders

Your loadout determines your survival odds more than any other decision. The nmrih2 tutorial recommends a balanced approach: one sidearm, one melee weapon, and one throwable. Here are three proven loadouts for different playstyles:

| Loadout | Sidearm | Melee | Throwable | Best Map |
|---------|---------|-------|-----------|----------|
| Standard | M1911 | Crowbar | Molotov | Raven Rock |
| Heavy | M9A3 | Fire Axe | Satchel Charge | Flooded |
| Stealth | Gruber 922 | Sledgehammer | Flashbang | Nightmare |

Each loadout costs different amounts of **Credits**. The Standard loadout is the cheapest and best for learning map layouts. The Heavy loadout trades mobility for raw damage, making it ideal for **Flooded**'s close-quarters hallways. The Stealth loadout maximizes inventory efficiency and pairs well with the **Gruber 922**'s suppressor. For a deeper breakdown of weapon stats, check out our [firearm guide](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-firearm-guide/). The official [NMRIH2 loadout recommendations](https://www.nmrih2.com) suggest first-time responders always carry a single throwable, never two, to keep weight low and stamina regeneration high.

## Zombie Types and How to Counter Them

Understanding zombie behavior is central to any nmrih2 tutorial. Each enemy type requires a different response, and mixing up your tactics will get you killed. Here's the complete breakdown:

| Type | Audio Cue | Health | Counter |
|------|-----------|--------|---------|
| Walker | Foot-drag shuffle | Low | Melee headshot |
| Runner | Heavy panting | Medium | Kite, don't fight |
| Shambler | Guttural scream | High | Focus fire |

**Walkers** are the most common enemy and the easiest to handle. A single **Sledgehammer** headshot kills them, and the **Crowbar** does the job in two swings. **Runners** are fast, aggressive, and dangerous in groups. The nmrih2 tutorial teaches you to kite them into doorways where teammates can finish them with shotguns like the **Fierra DB12**. **Shamblers** are tanky bullet sponges that require coordinated focus fire from rifles like the **M7A1**. Never engage a Shambler alone unless you have a clear escape route. When a Shambler screams, every zombie in the area gets alerted, so treat that audio cue as a call to reposition, not to charge in.

## Progression: Credits, Merits, and Gene Therapy

NMRIH2's progression system rewards smart play and punishes recklessness. **Credits** are earned from completing objectives and extracting successfully. They're spent on loadouts, equipment, and cosmetic items. **Merits** are earned from achieving specific milestones — like surviving your first **Nightmare** run — and unlock permanent perks that carry across all Responders. **Gene Therapy** is the most precious resource: it allows you to permanently boost a Responder's stats, but it's extremely limited.

| System | Earned From | Spent On |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Credits | Objectives, extractions | Loadouts, equipment |
| Merits | Milestones, challenges | Perks, cosmetics |
| Gene Therapy | Level-ups, rare drops | Permanent stat boosts |

One common mistake is wasting **Gene Therapy** on a low-level Responder. Save it for a character you plan to play long-term. The **NPV infection** mechanic adds another layer of strategy: if a Responder gets infected, you must use **Phalanx pills** to suppress symptoms until extraction. The pills don't cure the infection — they buy time. If you're new, read our [beginner guide](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-beginner-guide/) before spending any **Gene Therapy**. Understanding when to invest in a Responder versus when to retire them is a skill that separates veterans from fresh corpses.

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

Every nmrih2 tutorial should warn you about the traps that catch new players. Here are the top mistakes we see in public lobbies:

- Wasting **Gene Therapy** on low-level Responders

- Ignoring zombie audio cues and getting flanked

- Overencumbering inventory with unnecessary gear

- Fighting **Runners** head-on instead of kiting

- Breaking barricades unnecessarily for loot

Breaking barricades is the subtlest mistake. Barricades slow zombies and funnel them into kill zones. Destroying them for a few supplies often isn't worth the risk. The nmrih2 tutorial gym doesn't teach this, but experienced players know that barricade integrity is a team resource. Another common error is hoarding **Phalanx pills** instead of distributing them to infected teammates. An infected Responder is a ticking clock; share your supplies. The [Steam beginner survival guide](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/292000) specifically calls out wasting **Gene Therapy** and ignoring audio cues as the top two mistakes new players make.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does the nmrih2 tutorial take?**

The Tutorial Gym takes about 15 minutes to complete. It covers movement, melee, firearms, equipment, and audio identification. After finishing, you'll have enough **Credits** for your first standard loadout.

**What is the best weapon for beginners?**

The **M7A1** carbine is the best starting firearm due to its low recoil and high magazine capacity. Pair it with a **Crowbar** for silent melee kills and a **Molotov** for crowd control.

**Can you play No More Room in Hell 2 solo?**

Yes, but the game is balanced for co-op. Solo play requires more careful resource management and aggressive kiting. The nmrih2 tutorial recommends co-op for new players. Check our [how to survive guide](/en/guides/no-more-room-in-hell-2-how-to-survive/) for solo tactics.

**What are Phalanx pills used for?**

**Phalanx pills** suppress **NPV infection** symptoms temporarily. They don't cure the infection, so you still need to reach extraction before the infection progresses. Distribute them to infected teammates.

**How do I revive a teammate?**

You need a revive syringe. Approach the downed Responder and hold the interact button. The nmrih2 tutorial covers this in the equipment station.
