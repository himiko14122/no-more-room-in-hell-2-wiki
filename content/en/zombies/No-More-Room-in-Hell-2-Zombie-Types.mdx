## The Seven Core Zombie Types in No More Room in Hell 2

The Armageddon 1.0 update formalized the infected roster into seven distinct classes. Every no more room in hell 2 zombie types guide worth reading starts with the same foundation: the **Walker**. Walkers are the default infected, shambling toward you at a steady pace with roughly 100 health points. They stagger on headshots and drop after a single well-placed round from the **M7A1** carbine. Their attack windup is telegraphed, giving you a 0.8-second window to parry or sidestep.

### Walker: The Baseline Threat

The no more room in hell 2 walker zombie is the most common enemy you will encounter across all three difficulty settings. Walkers move at a walking pace but break into a brief jog when they have direct line of sight. They deal 15 damage per hit on Normal difficulty, which jumps to 30 on Nightmare. A group of five or more Walkers can pin you against a wall, so never let them close the gap. Use melee weapons like the **Sledgehammer** to conserve ammunition, but remember that every swing generates noise that attracts more Walkers from adjacent rooms.

### Runner and Prime Runner

The no more room in hell 2 runner zombie is the first major threat spike. Runners sprint at roughly 2.5 times the speed of a Walker and close distance in seconds. They have 60 health, making them fragile but dangerous in packs. A single headshot from the **Gruber 922** pistol drops a Runner instantly, but body shots require three to four rounds. The no more room in hell 2 prime runner is a tougher variant introduced in the 1.0 update, with 120 health and a 15% faster sprint speed. Prime Runners also resist stagger, meaning you cannot interrupt their lunge with a pistol round. Shotguns like the **Fierra DB12** are the most reliable counter because the spread guarantees limb damage.

### Crawler and Shambler

Crawlers are Runners that have lost both legs to dismemberment. They move at a crawl but maintain a low profile that makes headshots difficult from standing height. The no more room in hell 2 crawler zombie can still bite your ankles for 10 damage and will grab you if you walk past without checking the floor. **Shamblers** are the semi-rare variant that appears when a Walker is damaged but not killed; they move slower but have a larger attack radius. Both types are best handled with a stomp or a downward Sledgehammer swing rather than wasting ammunition.

### Armored and National Guard Zombies

The no more room in hell 2 armored zombies category includes National Guard infected that spawn on Flooded and Nightmare difficulties. These zombies wear ballistic vests and helmets that absorb 50% of bullet damage to the torso and 75% to the head. The no more room in hell 2 national guard zombie requires either a shotgun blast to the exposed jaw or a melee swing to the legs to bring down. Armored zombies also resist fire damage, so Molotovs are less effective against them than against standard Walkers.

### Special Infected Variants

Beyond the core seven, special variants like the **Chef zombie** appear in specific scenarios. The Chef wields a cleaver and has a faster attack speed than any standard melee zombie. ## Zombie AI Detection: Sight, Sound, and Blood Trails

Understanding how no more room in hell 2 zombie types perceive the world is the difference between stealth and a full-scale horde. According to the official [NMRIH2 detection mechanics documentation](https://www.nmrih2.com), zombies use three detection channels: sight, sound, and blood trails.

### Sound-Based Detection

Sound is the primary trigger for horde aggravation. Sprinting generates 30 noise units, melee swings generate 15, firearm shots generate 50 to 80 depending on the weapon, and explosions generate 150. Zombies within a 20-meter radius of any noise source will investigate the location. The M7A1 with a suppressor reduces gunshot noise to 20 units, making it the best stealth option for clearing rooms without triggering a horde.

### Sight and Blood Trail Detection

Sight detection operates on a 45-degree cone in front of each zombie, extending 15 meters for Walkers and 25 meters for Runners. Crouching reduces your visibility radius by half. Blood trails from injured Responders leave a scent marker that persists for 30 seconds; any zombie that crosses the trail will follow it to your last known position. This makes the **Phalanx pill** healing effect doubly important, as it stops bleeding and removes the trail.

## Dismemberment and Limb Damage Tactics

The limb damage system is the most unique mechanic in no more room in hell 2 zombie types combat. Every zombie has separate health pools for the head, torso, left arm, right arm, and both legs. Destroying a limb produces a permanent debuff that persists for the rest of that zombie's existence.

### Headshot Priority

Headshots deal 3x damage to all zombie types and instantly kill Walkers and Shamblers. Runners survive a headshot only if the round fails to penetrate; the M7A1's 5.56 rounds penetrate skulls reliably, while the Gruber 922's 9mm rounds sometimes deflect at angles. Prime Runners require two headshots unless you are using the Fierra DB12 at point-blank range, where the pellet spread guarantees brain trauma.

### Leg Shots and Crawler Conversion

Shooting out both legs of a Runner converts it into a Crawler, which is far easier to manage. This is a deliberate strategy for conserving ammunition during horde events. A single shotgun blast to the legs from the Fierra DB12 will drop most Runners into Crawler state. The tradeoff is that Crawlers still trigger noise when they grab you, so you must finish them with a stomp.

### Arm Shots and Attack Speed Reduction

Destroying one arm reduces a zombie's attack speed by 40%, and destroying both arms prevents it from attacking entirely. This is the safest way to neutralize a no more room in hell 2 national guard zombie without exposing yourself to its bite. Melee weapons like the Sledgehammer excel at arm removal because the heavy swing has a 70% chance to sever a limb outright. Horde waves in no more room in hell 2 zombie types are not random. The game uses a director system that tracks three triggers: scenario objectives, elapsed time, and accumulated noise. ### Trigger Conditions

Each map has scripted horde events tied to objective completion. For example, activating the generator on Raven Rock spawns a 12-zombie wave that includes two Prime Runners. Survival mode in the 1.0 update adds speaker-based horde spawns, where a malfunctioning speaker attracts zombies until you disable it. Time-based triggers occur every 90 seconds on Flooded difficulty, spawning a small patrol of three to five Walkers near the objective.

### Chokepoint Design

The most effective horde management tactic is designating a chokepoint before triggering an objective. Doorways, stairwells, and narrow corridors funnel zombies into a single-file line, letting one Responder with a Sledgehammer hold the line while teammates reload. ## Weapon Loadout Recommendations by Zombie Type

Your loadout should adapt to the no more room in hell 2 zombie types you expect to face on each map. The M7A1 carbine is the generalist's choice, offering reliable penetration and a 30-round magazine. The Fierra DB12 shotgun is mandatory for Nightmare runs because its spread handles Prime Runners and armored zombies efficiently. The Gruber 922 pistol serves as a lightweight backup that can still one-shot a Runner with a headshot.

### Firearm Recommendations

For mixed hordes, carry the M7A1 with a suppressor and a Fierra DB12 as your secondary. The suppressor keeps noise under 20 units, letting you clear Walkers without attracting the entire building. When a Prime Runner breaks through, swap to the shotgun and aim for the legs to convert it into a Crawler. Ammunition conservation matters; each magazine of M7A1 rounds costs 50 Credits from the CRC supply locker, so melee kills on lone Walkers are essential to stretch your budget.

### Melee Weapon Recommendations

The Sledgehammer is the best melee weapon in the game for zombie type control. Its heavy swing has a 70% limb sever chance and can one-shot a Walker with a headshot. The tradeoff is stamina: each heavy swing consumes 25% of your stamina bar, so you cannot spam it. Pair the Sledgehammer with the Phalanx pill for stamina regeneration, and use the Gruber 922 for emergency Runners that close distance while you are mid-swing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the strongest zombie type in No More Room in Hell 2?**

The Prime Runner is the strongest standard zombie, with 120 health and a 15% faster sprint speed than regular Runners. Armored National Guard zombies are tougher but slower. On Nightmare difficulty, a Prime Runner can down an unarmored Responder in two hits, so always prioritize them with shotgun blasts.

**How do I turn a Runner into a Crawler?**

Shoot both legs with any weapon. A single Fierra DB12 blast at close range will sever both legs instantly. The resulting Crawler moves slowly and can be finished with a stomp. This conserves ammunition and reduces the threat level during horde events.

**Do headshots always kill zombies instantly?**

No. Walkers and Shamblers die from any headshot, but Runners survive if the bullet deflects, and Prime Runners require two headshots. Armored National Guard zombies need a shot to the exposed jaw area. The M7A1 carbine has the most reliable penetration for headshots.

**What attracts zombies in NMRIH2?**

Zombies detect you through sight, sound, and blood trails. Gunshots generate 50-80 noise units, sprinting generates 30, and explosions generate 150. Blood trails from injuries persist for 30 seconds. Use suppressed weapons and Phalanx pills to minimize detection.

**Are there special zombies beyond the seven core types?**

Yes. Chef zombies appear in specific scenarios and have faster attack speeds, while police zombies retain body armor and require flanking.
