## Lewiston Map Structure and Threat Flow

Lewiston's layout follows a classic suburban grid with three main avenues running parallel to each other, connected by residential side streets that create natural chokepoints. The map's objective flow typically pushes squads from the outer residential ring toward the central commercial strip, where the final extraction point sits behind a defensible barricade line. Because the map is so open compared to indoor scenarios, sound discipline becomes a survival mechanic rather than a tactical nicety — gunfire echoes through the streets and pulls zombies from adjacent blocks.

The **threat density** on Lewiston spikes in predictable locations, which means experienced squads can pre-position before the horde arrives. The schoolyard at the northeast corner, the gas station on the west avenue, and the church interior all serve as major spawn anchors that feed zombies into the main avenues. Community reports indicate that the gas station area alone accounts for roughly a third of the total zombie population on any given run, making it a high-value but high-risk loot zone that teams should clear early or bypass entirely.

## Loadout Priorities for Lewiston

Your weapon selection on Lewiston needs to account for the map's mixed engagement ranges, where you'll face both long-distance threats across open streets and sudden close-quarters encounters inside buildings. The **M7A1 rifle** remains the community's top pick for this map because its high-capacity magazine lets you sustain suppressive fire during horde waves without constant reloads, and its iron sights are clean enough for medium-range precision work. Players who prefer a more mobile playstyle often swap to the submachine gun class, but the reduced damage per shot means you'll burn through ammunition faster during extended firefights.

The secondary slot deserves just as much consideration as your primary, because Lewiston's building interiors force frequent weapon transitions. A suppressed sidearm is the standard recommendation for clearing rooms quietly, since unsuppressed gunfire inside enclosed spaces pulls zombies from several blocks away. The **Sledgehammer** remains the melee weapon of choice for this map because its wide swing arc handles multiple targets in tight hallways, and it never runs out of ammunition — a critical advantage when supply runs go wrong.

Ammunition management on Lewiston is a squad-wide responsibility rather than an individual concern, because the map's open layout encourages sustained firefights that drain reserves quickly. Veterans recommend assigning one player to carry the **supply pack** role, which means prioritizing inventory space for ammo boxes and medical supplies over personal weapon upgrades. This player should stay in the middle of the formation during movement, ready to distribute resources when the squad stops to consolidate between objectives.

The **armor situation** on Lewiston deserves special attention because the map's zombie density means you'll take hits even with good positioning. Light armor offers better mobility for the route-running playstyle that suits this map, but medium armor provides meaningful protection during the final extraction defense when the horde converges from all directions. Community testing suggests that the mobility trade-off is worth it for most of the run, but squads should coordinate a gear swap before the final objective if they have spare armor in their supply cache.

## Team Roles and Formation Strategy

Successful Lewiston runs depend on a clear division of labor, and the map's open layout makes role discipline more important than on corridor-heavy maps where everyone can see the same threats. The standard formation uses a **pointman** who scouts ahead and identifies zombie clusters before they become ambushes, while the **rear guard** watches the squad's back against the constant trickle of zombies that follow from cleared areas. The two support players hold the middle, covering the flanks and managing the supply pack.

The pointman role on Lewiston requires a specific loadout that prioritizes mobility and perception over raw damage. A lightweight primary like the MP5 variant, a suppressed sidearm for quiet scouting, and light armor give the pointman the speed to pull back when they spot a large cluster. This player should also carry a flare or similar signaling tool to mark dangerous areas for the rest of the squad without using voice chat, which can attract zombies if used too loudly.

The support player with the shotgun provides close-range protection for the supply pack carrier, and their weapon's spread makes it effective for clearing rooms during building searches.

The **rear guard** role is often underestimated on Lewiston, but the map's open layout means zombies constantly filter in from behind as you advance. This player needs the situational awareness to detect flanking movements and the firepower to hold off a sudden surge while the rest of the squad completes an objective. The sledgehammer in their secondary slot gives them a silent option for dispatching the occasional straggler without drawing more zombies, which keeps the squad's overall noise profile lower during stealth sections.

## Broadway and Powerplant Comparisons

Understanding how Lewiston differs from the other objective maps helps you adapt your loadout and strategy when you rotate through the map pool. The **Broadway map** shifts the setting to an urban commercial district with taller buildings and narrower streets, which creates more vertical sightlines and tighter engagement ranges than Lewiston's suburban sprawl. This means the long-range rifle builds that work well on Lewiston become less effective on Broadway, where close-quarters combat dominates and shotguns gain significant value.

The **Powerplant map** presents the opposite challenge, with industrial facilities that feature long, straight corridors and large open assembly floors. This layout actually rewards the same long-range loadouts that work on Lewiston, but the map's industrial aesthetic creates more hard cover points and fewer open sightlines than the suburban streets. Players who master Lewiston's long-range engagement patterns will find the transition to Powerplant relatively smooth, while Broadway requires a more significant loadout shift toward close-quarters weapons.
