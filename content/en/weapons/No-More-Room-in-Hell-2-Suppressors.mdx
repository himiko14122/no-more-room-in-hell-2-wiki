# No More Room in Hell 2 Suppressors Explained Guide

Suppressors in **No More Room in Hell 2** are among the most valuable attachments you can find in a run. Unlike traditional shooters where a suppressor simply reduces your sound signature, the **NMRIH2 suppressor guide** community has proven that these tubes fundamentally change how you engage the undead. Every gunshot in **No More Room in Hell 2** generates noise that travels through walls, around corners, and across floors, alerting every **Walker**, **Runner**, and **Shambler** in the vicinity. A single unsuppressed shot from an **M7A1** can pull an entire horde from two floors away, while a suppressed shot might only wake the zombies in the same room. This guide covers everything you need about **no more room in hell 2 suppressors**, from spawn locations to loadout synergies, and explains why stealth is often the difference between a successful extraction and a messy death.

The attachment system in NMRIH2 is deeper than most players expect. Suppressors compete with muzzle brakes, compensators, and flash hiders for the same slot on your weapon. While a muzzle brake helps control recoil during sustained fire, a suppressor offers a unique tactical advantage: the ability to clear rooms without triggering a chain reaction of zombie aggro. In **Nightmare** difficulty, where **Permadeath** is always active, that advantage becomes critical. Veterans who have survived the **Raven Rock** and **Flooded** maps will tell you that a suppressed **Gruber 922** is worth more than a full-auto rifle without one. Let's break down the mechanics, the best host weapons, and the hidden nuances that most guides miss.

## How Suppressors Work in NMRIH2

Suppressors in **No More Room in Hell 2** do not eliminate gunshot sound entirely; they reduce the audible radius by roughly 70% and change how zombies react to the noise. When you fire an unsuppressed weapon, the game creates a loud noise event with a radius that scales with caliber and barrel length. A suppressed shot creates a smaller, less penetrating noise event. This means zombies outside the reduced radius will not investigate, but those inside will still converge on your position. The **CRC** (Crisis Response Committee) field manual describes this as "noise attenuation," and it is the single most important stat to understand when building a stealth loadout.

### Sound Radius and Zombie Aggro

The exact numbers are not shown in the UI, but community testing has mapped the behavior. A suppressed **M7A1** shot has a sound radius of about 15 meters, while the same rifle unsuppressed reaches 40 meters. Walls reduce the radius further, but only if the wall is solid and has no open doorways. Zombies in NMRIH2 do not simply path toward the sound; they enter a "search" state and wander toward the last known position. This gives you a small window to relocate before they reach your firing position. Against **Runners**, the window is much smaller because they sprint directly to the noise source. Suppressors are therefore most effective when you are clearing **Shamblers** and **Walkers** in close quarters, where the reduced aggro radius lets you dispatch them one by one.

### Damage and Recoil Trade-offs

Many players assume that a suppressor reduces damage, but in **No More Room in Hell 2**, the damage penalty is negligible—around 5% on most weapons. The real trade-off is recoil control. Suppressors add weight to the front of the gun, increasing recoil by roughly 10% and slowing your aim-down-sights speed slightly. For semi-automatic weapons like the **Gruber 922**, this is barely noticeable. For full-auto rifles like the **MC-15**, you will feel the muzzle climb after the third round. The **NMRIH2 suppressor guide** community recommends pairing suppressors with a foregrip or a vertical grip to offset the recoil penalty. If you find a suppressor early, consider whether your current weapon benefits more from a muzzle brake or a compensator, especially if you are playing a loud support role.

## Best Weapons for Suppressors

Not every weapon can accept a suppressor, and even among those that can, some are far better hosts than others. The attachment system in NMRIH2 is weapon-specific, so you cannot swap a suppressor from an **M7A1** to a **Fierra DB12** shotgun. The following weapons are the most effective suppressor hosts in the current meta.

### M7A1 Rifle

The **M7A1** is the workhorse of the **Responder** class and arguably the best suppressor host in the game. Its 5.56mm rounds are common, its semi-auto mode is accurate, and the suppressor reduces its already moderate sound signature to a whisper. With a suppressor attached, the **M7A1** becomes a precision tool for headshotting **Walkers** from behind cover without alerting the rest of the horde. The only downside is that the **M7A1** has a slower rate of fire than the **MC-15**, so you need to make every shot count. Pair it with a 4x scope and you have a DMR that can clear an entire floor of **Raven Rock** without ever firing a second unsuppressed round.

### MC-15 Carbine

The **MC-15** is the full-auto alternative, and it benefits from a suppressor in a different way. While the recoil penalty is noticeable, the ability to fire short bursts without drawing the entire map is invaluable during objective defense. The **MC-15** with a suppressor is the go-to choice for players who expect to encounter **Runners** in tight corridors. You can drop a **Runner** with a three-round burst and immediately move to a new position before the noise attracts more zombies. The **MC-15** also accepts a wide range of optics, making it a flexible platform for both stealth and loud playstyles.

### Gruber 922 Pistol

The **Gruber 922** is a small-caliber pistol that is often overlooked, but with a suppressor it becomes the ultimate stealth sidearm. It uses common 9mm ammo, has low recoil, and its suppressed sound radius is so small that you can fire it in a room with a door closed without alerting zombies on the other side. The **Gruber 922** is the perfect backup for a **Sledgehammer** main weapon. When you need to break a line of sight or take out a single **Shambler** without dropping your melee weapon, the suppressed **Gruber 922** is your best friend. It also pairs well with the **Gene Therapy** perk, which increases your healing efficiency, because you will take fewer hits when you are not drawing hordes.

## Where to Find Suppressors

Suppressors are rare attachments in **No More Room in Hell 2**. They do not spawn in every map, and when they do, they are usually in high-risk locations. Knowing where to look can save you hours of fruitless searching.

### Map Spawns: Raven Rock and Flooded

The **Raven Rock** map has the highest concentration of suppressor spawns. Check the armory lockers in the basement, the security office on the second floor, and the maintenance tunnels. The **Flooded** map also has a few spawns, primarily in the police station and the flooded subway maintenance rooms. In both maps, suppressors are more likely to appear on higher difficulty settings. On **Nightmare** difficulty, you can find them in loot containers that are guarded by **Runners** or placed in areas that require a keycard to access. If you are playing solo, prioritize these locations early in the run, because other players will grab them quickly.

### Loot Tiers and Rarity

Suppressors belong to the "tier 3" loot category, along with high-tier optics and extended magazines. The drop rate is roughly 2% from standard containers and 8% from locked containers. You can increase your chances by using the **Phalanx** class perk that boosts rare loot spawns, but this comes at the cost of other combat perks. The **NMRIH2 ammo guide** community has noted that suppressors are more common on maps with a military or police theme, so plan your route accordingly. If you find a suppressor but no compatible weapon, hold onto it—you can attach it later when you find an **M7A1** or **MC-15**.

## Suppressor vs Other Attachments

Choosing between a suppressor and other muzzle attachments is a matter of playstyle, but the current meta heavily favors suppressors for objective-based runs. The table below compares the three main muzzle attachments.

| Attachment | Sound Radius | Recoil | Damage | Best Use Case |
|------------|--------------|--------|--------|---------------|
| **Suppressor** | Reduced by 70% | +10% | -5% | Stealth, Nightmare, solo |
| **Muzzle Brake** | No change | -20% | 0% | Full-auto, loud support |
| **Compensator** | No change | -15% vertical | 0% | Mid-range, burst fire |
| **Flash Hider** | No change | -5% | 0% | PvP, visual concealment |

As you can see, the suppressor is the only attachment that reduces your sound signature. In a game where sound is the primary threat, that alone makes it the best choice for most situations. However, if you are playing a loud build with a **Fierra DB12** shotgun and a **Sledgehammer**, you might prefer a muzzle brake to keep your shotgun on target during rapid follow-up shots. The **Fierra DB12** cannot accept a suppressor anyway, so you should reserve suppressors for your primary rifle or sidearm.

## Tactical Use: Stealth and Nightmare Mode

The true value of **no more room in hell 2 suppressors** emerges in **Nightmare** mode, where **Permadeath** is always enabled and every mistake is permanent. In this mode, drawing a horde is often a death sentence. A suppressed weapon lets you engage zombies from a distance, thin out groups, and create safe paths for your team. The **CRC** recommends a "shoot and move" doctrine: fire one or two shots, relocate, and observe. This works exceptionally well with a suppressor because the reduced sound radius means zombies will investigate your last position, not your current one.

### Avoiding Walkers, Runners, and Shamblers

Different zombie types react differently to suppressed gunfire. **Walkers** are slow and will wander toward the noise, giving you plenty of time to reposition. **Shamblers** are slightly faster and more aggressive, but still manageable. **Runners** are the real threat—they sprint directly to the sound and can close 15 meters in under two seconds. When you hear a **Runner** nearby, stop firing and switch to melee or use a **molotov** to create a barrier. The **no more room in hell 2 molotov** is an excellent complement to a suppressor because it creates a loud fire that masks your movement while burning zombies that get too close. Use the molotov to funnel zombies into a choke point, then pick them off with your suppressed rifle.

### Permadeath and Team Coordination

In **Permadeath** runs, communication is everything. If one player fires an unsuppressed weapon, the entire team suffers the consequences. The **NMRIH2 suppressor guide** community has developed a simple rule: "No unsuppressed shots unless the horde is already on us." This ensures that the team can move through maps like **Raven Rock** and **Flooded** without triggering unnecessary fights. If you are the only player with a suppressor, take point and clear the path. Your teammates should hold their fire and follow your lead. When you encounter a **Runner**, call it out and let the player with the suppressor take the shot. This level of coordination is what separates successful **Nightmare** runs from quick deaths.

### Credits and Merits

Suppressors are not purchasable with **Credits** or **Merits** in the current build. They are exclusively found in-world, which makes them even more valuable. If you have a surplus of **Credits**, spend them on ammo and medical supplies instead, because a suppressor is only useful if you have the resources to survive the run. **Merits** are earned by completing objectives and are used to unlock permanent perks. The **Phalanx** perk tree has a node that increases rare loot spawns, and it is worth investing in if you want to find more suppressors. However, do not neglect **Gene Therapy**, which improves your healing and is essential for **Nightmare** runs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do suppressors reduce damage in No More Room in Hell 2?**
Suppressors reduce damage by about 5%, which is negligible for most weapons. The real trade-off is increased recoil and slower aim-down-sights speed. For semi-automatic weapons like the Gruber 922, the damage penalty is barely noticeable, and the stealth benefit far outweighs the cost.

**Can you attach a suppressor to any weapon?**
No, suppressors are weapon-specific. They can be attached to the M7A1, MC-15, Gruber 922, and a few other rifles and pistols. Shotguns like the Fierra DB12 and most melee weapons cannot accept suppressors. Always check the attachment compatibility before picking up a suppressor.

**Where is the best place to find suppressors?**
The Raven Rock map has the highest number of suppressor spawns, especially in armory lockers and security offices. The Flooded map also has spawns in the police station and maintenance rooms. Higher difficulties and locked containers increase your chances of finding one.

**Are suppressors worth using in Nightmare mode?**
Yes, suppressors are almost mandatory in Nightmare mode because Permadeath makes every mistake permanent. The reduced sound radius lets you clear zombies without drawing a horde, which is essential for survival. Pair a suppressor with a molotov to control crowds and create escape routes.

**Do suppressors affect zombie AI behavior?**
Suppressors reduce the sound radius, so zombies outside that radius will not react. Zombies inside the radius will still investigate, but they will be slower to pinpoint your exact position. This gives you time to relocate, which is especially useful against Runners.
